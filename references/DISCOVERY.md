# Product and ICP discovery

Use this capability when the product, offer, audience, or qualification criteria are unclear. Stop at a useful discovery result; contact lookup is a separate need.

## Decision inputs

Reuse product descriptions, URLs, existing positioning, known limitations, real case studies, and explicit business goals. If browsing is unavailable, analyze supplied content and say the website was not checked. Website copy is evidence of what the seller claims, not independent proof of performance.

## Judgment

Describe what is sold, who uses it, who might decide or pay, the problem it plausibly addresses, and what the offer actually includes. Separate verified features from claimed benefits and unknown commercial details. Do not fabricate pricing, integration support, certifications, customers, quantified outcomes, or guarantees.

Form a small number of useful ICP hypotheses, not a compulsory three segments. For each, identify fit signals, likely role, disqualifiers, a plausible relevance argument, and missing evidence. A hiring page can support a hiring observation; it does not prove an urgent software purchase. Use dated signals carefully.

If the offer is too vague to draft credibly, state the gap and propose a clearer offer using only supported capabilities. A weak offer does not justify sending more messages.

## Output

Adapt to the request. A compact product brief and segment table are usually sufficient:

`segment | supported fit | hypothesis | exclusion | unknown | next check`

Sources belong beside consequential claims or in a short evidence list. For provided private material, use its file or record reference rather than uploading it elsewhere.

## Example (fictional)

A supplied brief says a tool exports QA checklists to Markdown. A suitable hypothesis is that small QA teams may value portable review artifacts. Do not say it reduces defects by 40%, integrates with Jira, or is already used by the prospect unless separate evidence supports those claims.
